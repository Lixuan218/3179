From Gallery to Gallery:

Australia’s Visual Heritage

Exploring Australia's visual heritage through its public galleries, collections, artistic periods and cultural stories.

Introduction

Australia’s public galleries preserve more than artworks.

Public galleries play an important role in preserving and presenting Australia’s visual heritage. Their collections bring together artworks from different places, periods and cultural traditions, creating a record of how Australian art and identity have developed over time.

This visualisation explores Australia’s public gallery landscape from several perspectives: where galleries are located, what kinds of institutions make up the sector, how collections are structured, and how artworks reflect different periods and cultural stories.

Guiding question

What can Australia’s public galleries and their collections reveal about the country’s visual heritage?

Gallery network

Australia's public gallery network extends across all eight states and territories, but its geographical distribution is uneven. According to the Australian Public Galleries Snapshot 2019, Queensland recorded the highest estimated number of small-to-medium public galleries (104), followed by New South Wales (96) and Victoria (78). In contrast, Tasmania (11) and the Northern Territory (8) recorded substantially fewer galleries. These differences highlight the varied scale of public gallery networks across Australia. However, gallery counts alone do not indicate the size of collections or how accessible galleries are to local communities.

What types of public galleries make up the sector?

Public and regional galleries dominate the sector, while contemporary art spaces, art museums, craft galleries and Aboriginal art centres make up smaller shares.

Gallery network

Australia’s public gallery sector is broad in scale and diverse in structure. Public and regional galleries form the dominant institutional type, showing that Australia’s visual heritage is supported not only by major museums but also by an extensive network of local and regional institutions.

Sector structure

The sector includes several different types of cultural institutions, from public and regional galleries to contemporary art spaces, art museums, craft centres and Aboriginal art centres. Together, these institutions provide different ways for artworks and cultural material to be collected, exhibited and shared.

Section 04 · Inside the collection

What is preserved inside these collections?

After comparing the collecting identities of major Australian galleries, this section looks more closely at how collections are preserved, accessed and represented through a case study of the QAGOMA Collection.

Permanent collections

How many galleries hold a permanent collection?

Nearly three-quarters of surveyed galleries hold a permanent collection. Each square represents one percentage point.

What this shows

Permanent collections remain central to the public gallery sector.

Around 72% of surveyed galleries report holding a permanent collection, while 28% do not. This suggests that most public galleries play a long-term custodial role in preserving artworks and cultural material rather than functioning only as temporary exhibition spaces.

72% hold a permanent collection
Collection access

How accessible are these collections?

Preserving artworks is only one part of collection management. Digitisation helps galleries document their holdings, while online access allows audiences to explore collections beyond the physical gallery.

From preservation to access

Digitisation does not always mean public accessibility.

While 74% of collection material is digitised, only 35% is available online. This reveals a substantial gap between documenting collections internally and making them accessible to wider audiences.

39 percentage-point gap between digitisation and online access

A few categories dominate, but the collection extends much further.

The largest categories account for a substantial share of the collection, but smaller categories such as jewellery, installation, metalwork, furniture, film and leatherwork demonstrate a much wider range of artistic forms.

The cumulative line makes this concentration visible. As categories are added from largest to smallest, the line approaches 100%, showing how both major and minor categories contribute to the overall collection.

The bigger picture

Australia’s public galleries do more than display artworks. Through permanent collections, digitisation and the preservation of many different artistic forms, galleries act as long-term custodians of visual culture and help carry Australia’s artistic heritage into the future.
